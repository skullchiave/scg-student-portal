Before you apply

These rules come from immigration, not from the school.

You may work 28 hours per week at most. During long school holidays the limit is 8 hours per day. You need a work permit (資格外活動許可) on your residence card. Some jobs are not allowed at all — bars, pachinko, and similar places.
Ask the office first. Bring the job advertisement. A teacher will check the hours and the type of work with you before you apply. It takes 5 minutes and it has saved students their visa.

Senior Voices

Graduates answer the same six questions, so you can compare schools fairly. These are people who sat in your classroom one or two years ago.

InterviewsUpdated 2026-07-15
A Graduate AIT vocational school, Osaka — 2nd year
What surprised you
Almost every class is group work. I thought I would just listen and take notes, but I have to speak Japanese every day.
Money outside tuition
About 40,000 yen for a laptop and textbooks in the first month.
Japanese you really need
N2 is written in the requirements, but N3 level conversation is enough for the first term. Reading the textbooks is the hard part.
What I should have done first
Learned to touch-type in Japanese. Everyone else was faster than me for six months.
B Graduate BHotel & tourism school, Kyoto — 1st year
What surprised you
There is a uniform and a hair rule. Nobody told me before I applied.
A day in my life
08:40 school, 15:30 finish, 17:00–21:00 part-time job. Wednesday is free.
Part-time work
Possible, but not in the internship month. Save money before that month.
What I should have done first
Practising keigo. I only learned it two weeks before the interview.
C Graduate CCare work college, Osaka — 2nd year
What surprised you
The school found my job for me in the first year. I did not have to look.
Money outside tuition
Almost nothing. Uniform 12,000 yen, and the practice shoes.
Japanese you really need
Listening matters most. Old people speak quietly and use dialect. Reading is easier than I feared.
What I should have done first
Visited a care home once before deciding. It is a job you should see first.
D Graduate DUniversity, Faculty of Business — 1st year
What surprised you
Nobody checks if you come. You can fail without anyone telling you.
A day in my life
Three or four classes a day, and long gaps between them. I use the gaps in the library.
Japanese you really need
Writing. Every class ends with a report of 800–1,200 characters.
What I should have done first
The EJU practice, much earlier. I started three months before and that was late.
E Graduate EWorking — food company, Osaka — 2nd year
What surprised you
The interview was not about Japanese. They asked why I chose Japan and what I want in 5 years.
Japanese you really need
N2 was the line to apply. At work, the hard part is the telephone.
Money
First salary 195,000 yen before tax. Rent 58,000 yen. It is enough, but not much.
What I should have done first
Started looking one year before graduation. Companies close applications very early.
